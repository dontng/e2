## 原句

> The phrase “less is more” was actually first popularized by a German, the architect Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, who like other people associated with the Bauhaus, a school of design, emigrated to the United States before World War Ⅱ and took up posts at American architecture schools.

## 我的理解和翻译

术语少就是多，实际上是由一个德国人第一个流行开来的，是设计师Ludwig Mies van der Rohe，其他被组织的人倾向于称之为the Bauhaus，这是一种学院派的设计，二战前迁徙到美国，并在美国建造学校拿下很多支持

## 批改

```
术语少就是多，实际上是由一个德国人第一个流行开来的，是设计师Ludwig Mies
van der Rohe，其他被组织的人倾向于称之为the Bauhaus，这是一种学院派的设计，
二战前迁徙到美国，并在美国建造学校拿下很多支持
```

**[1] "less is more" → 术语少就是多**
断在词义锁死。"less is more" 是一句广为人知的设计格言，核心意思是"简约即美"/"少即是多"。硬译字面只得到一个没有意义的短语。这里是作为一个具名短语（加引号）出现的，翻译时保留引号、给出约定俗成的译法即可。

**[2] was first popularized by → 第一个流行开来的（主语偏移）**
断在被动语态的主语。原句主语是 the phrase "less is more"，不是人。被动结构 was popularized by a German 意思是"由一个德国人推广开来"，学生把 a German 挪成了主语，导致后面 the architect 的同位语关系也断掉了。

**[3] associated with → 被组织的人倾向于称之为**
断在词义极性。associate with = 与……有关联/联系，是中性的归属关系，不含"倾向于称之为"的意思。people associated with the Bauhaus 就是"与包豪斯有关联的人/包豪斯相关人士"。插入语 a school of design 是对 the Bauhaus 的解释性同位语，学生把它译成了"学院派的设计"，丢掉了"设计学院"这个定性。

**[4] took up posts → 拿下很多支持**
断在词义完全丢失。take up posts = 就任（某些）职位，是一个固定词块，posts 此处是"职位/教职"。学生读到 posts 可能联想到 post 的其他义项（支柱/帖子），再跟 architecture 组合后猜成了"建造支持"，路径是字形触发联想，不是语义推断。

**[5] architecture schools → 建造学校**
断在词义。architecture = 建筑学（作为学科），不是"建造"这个动作。American architecture schools = 美国的建筑学院。

## Vocab

**popularize** *v.*

词根 popular（来自拉丁语 populus，人民）加 -ize 使动后缀，整体意思是"使……在大众中流行/推广开来"。**核心意象：把一个小圈子里的东西推到大众视野里。**

1. Darwin's theories were first popularized in the late nineteenth century by writers who saw in evolution a justification for social inequality.
2. The architect who popularized open-plan living spaces argued that walls were psychological barriers as much as physical ones.
3. To *popularize* a concept is different from simply making it *popular*—the former implies an active agent doing the spreading, the latter only describes the end state.

---

**emigrate** *v.*

词根 e-（向外）+ migrare（迁移），指"从本国/本地离开，移居他处"，强调离开的一端。**核心意象：拎着行李从自己的国家走出去。**

1. Millions of Eastern Europeans emigrated to the industrial cities of North America in the decades before and after 1900, fundamentally reshaping the demographics of both regions.
2. When the political climate turned hostile, many of the country's most talented scientists emigrated, taking with them not just expertise but the networks that sustained it.
3. She *emigrated* from Poland to France, where she eventually *immigrated* and settled permanently—the difference is only about which end of the journey you're naming.

---

**take up posts** *v. phr.*

take up = 开始从事/接手，posts = 职位（尤指正式的学术或官方职务）。**核心意象：走进一个已经等在那里的位置，坐下来开始干活。**

1. After completing his fellowship at Oxford, he took up a post at the University of Chicago, where he would spend the next three decades.
2. Several members of the Bauhaus who emigrated before the war took up posts at American schools, effectively transplanting the modernist design curriculum into a new continent.
3. She *took up a post* as department chair, which meant she had to *give up* her research grant—you cannot hold both positions simultaneously.

---

**architecture** *n.*

来自希腊语 arkhitekton（arkhi- 首席 + tekton 建造者），-ure 是名词后缀表学科/技艺。**核心意象：用空间和结构思考的那门学问，不只是盖房子，是设计空间的哲学。**

1. The architecture of the new terminal was praised not only for its aesthetic boldness but for the way it managed the movement of thousands of passengers without friction.
2. A course in the history of architecture is, in effect, a course in the history of how societies imagine the relationship between people and built space.
3. *Architecture* (建筑学) is distinct from *construction* (建造/施工)—the former is a discipline concerned with design and theory, the latter with the physical act of building.

## Phrases

**associated with** *phr.*

结构：past participle 作后置定语，意思是"与……有关联的/隶属于……的"，常用来表示归属、关联、协作关系，不含评价色彩。**核心意象：一条细线把两样东西连在一起，不一定是正式成员，但有实质联系。**

1. Many of the scientists associated with the Manhattan Project later became vocal advocates for nuclear arms control, haunted by the consequences of their own work.
2. Critics associated with the Frankfurt School argued that mass culture did not liberate people but instead shaped their desires in ways that served the existing social order.
3. Being *associated with* a controversial figure is not the same as *being responsible for* their actions—the phrase signals connection, not complicity.

---

**a school of design** *appositive phr.*

同位语结构：名词短语紧跟在专有名词后面，用逗号隔开，功能是解释前面那个读者可能不熟悉的名词。**核心意象：括号里写的注解——"以防你不认识这个词，这里告诉你它是什么"。**

1. The Situationist International, a loose collective of artists and theorists, developed the concept of *détournement*—repurposing existing cultural materials to subvert their original meaning.
2. Her mentor, a professor of computational linguistics, had spent forty years studying how children acquire the grammar of their mother tongue without explicit instruction.
3. When a text inserts *an appositive phrase* directly after a proper noun, read it as the author's own gloss; when it inserts *a relative clause*, the author is adding new information rather than defining.
