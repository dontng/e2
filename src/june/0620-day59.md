## 批改

```
但是这些研究人员认为，外面的导演[1]，如果在坏的新闻播报[2]前离开公司，
会有很轻松的时期[3]避开blow对他们的报导[4]，甚至如果回顾历史，
表明那段做出糟糕执行[5]的时期期间，他们是在董事会的
```

**[1] outside directors → "外面的导演"**
断在词义层。`director` 在公司语境里是"董事"，不是影视里的"导演"。`outside director` 是专业术语"外部董事"——指不在公司任职的独立董事，区别于内部（executive）董事。词义被日常联想锁死了。

**[2] bad news breaks → "坏的新闻播报"**
断在词义极性。`break` 这里不是"播报/广播"，是"（消息）爆出/曝光"——新闻忽然被公众知道的那个节点。极性没反，但语义场偏了：播报是主动发布，break 是隐藏的事被曝出来。

**[3] have an easier time of avoiding → "有很轻松的时期"**
断在搭配识别。`have an easy/easier time of doing sth.` 是固定搭配，整体意思是"更容易做某事"，不是"有一段时期"。`time` 这里不是时间段，是"难易程度"——参考 `have a hard time doing`，同一结构。

**[4] a blow to their reputations → "blow 对他们的报导"**
两处断点叠加。① `blow to sth.` 是"对……的打击"，不是"报导"；② `reputation` 漏译了，应译"声誉/名誉"。结果这半句意思完全丢失。

**[5] wrongdoing → "糟糕的执行"**
断在词义极性。`wrongdoing` = wrong + doing，指"不当行为、违规行为、（道德或法律上的）错误行为"，带明确负面道德判断，不是中性的"执行"。这个词在法律/公司治理语境下接近"违规/失当行为"。

## Vocab

### outside director
*n.* 外部董事

`out-` 表示"在外部"，`director` 来自拉丁语 *dirigere*（引导、管理），原意是"给方向的人"。公司治理里，`outside director` 专指不受雇于公司、独立于管理层的董事会成员，区别于 `inside/executive director`（内部执行董事）。

**核心意象：** 坐在桌边但不住在这栋楼里的人——有发言权，但没有日常利益纠葛。

1. Outside directors are expected to bring independent judgment to board decisions, free from the conflicts of interest that can cloud the thinking of executives who depend on the company for their livelihood.
2. When the scandal broke, the outside directors who had resigned months earlier found themselves largely shielded from the regulatory inquiry that ensnared their former colleagues.
3. Unlike inside directors, who are typically full-time employees with stock options and bonuses tied to performance, outside directors receive a flat fee and are meant to act as a check on management.

---

### wrongdoing
*n.* 不当行为；违规行为；违法行为

`wrong`（错误的、不道德的）+ `doing`（所做之事）直接合成，无需词根分析。词义带明确的道德或法律否定判断，用于指个人或机构做了不该做的事——轻则违反规程，重则触犯法律。不同于 `mistake`（无意失误）或 `misconduct`（失职行为），`wrongdoing` 常暗示主观性和可追责性。

**核心意象：** 写进档案的那笔黑——不是犯错，是做了不该做的。

1. The investigation found no evidence of criminal wrongdoing, but the executives were nonetheless censured for breaching their fiduciary duties to shareholders.
2. Board members who stepped down before the accounting irregularities came to light were later shown by company records to have been present when the wrongdoing was sanctioned at the highest level.
3. While *mistake* suggests an unintentional error and *misconduct* focuses on professional failure, *wrongdoing* carries a heavier moral charge — it implies someone knew what they were doing was wrong.

---

### reputation
*n.* 声誉；名声

来自拉丁语 *reputatio*（反复思考、估量），词根 *re-*（反复）+ *putare*（思考、计算）。`reputation` 是别人反复观察后形成的评价结果——不是自我标榜，是外部积累的口碑。常见搭配：`damage/harm/protect/build one's reputation`；`reputation for sth.`（以……著称）。

**核心意象：** 你不在场时别人谈起你的样子。

1. A single fraud conviction can destroy a reputation built over decades, which is why even the appearance of impropriety can prompt executives to distance themselves from troubled companies.
2. By leaving the firm six months before the scandal broke, she preserved her reputation as a rigorous independent director — something her colleagues who stayed on could no longer claim.
3. Don't confuse *reputation* with *character*: character is who you are when no one is watching; reputation is what people believe about you when you're not in the room.

## Phrases

### have an easy/easier time of doing sth.
*固定搭配* 更容易做某事；做某事更轻松

结构：`have a(n) [easy/hard/difficult/rough] time of doing`，`time` 在这里不是时间段，是"难易体验"的量化——类比 `have a good time`（玩得开心）中 `time` 指一种主观感受，不是时钟时间。`of` 后接动名词，说明体验的具体内容。反义搭配 `have a hard time of doing` 更常见，帮助锚定这个结构。

**核心意象：** 这件事对你来说是顺水推舟还是逆水行舟。

1. Firms with strong internal controls have an easier time of detecting fraud early, before it has compounded into losses that threaten the whole enterprise.
2. Analysts who had covered the sector for years had an easier time of spotting the accounting irregularities than those who came to the story late.
3. Compare: *She had a hard time persuading the board to act* (= it was difficult) vs. *She had an easier time of it once the chairman stepped down* — the second uses "of it" as a shorthand when the action is already understood.

---

### a blow to sth.
*名词词块* 对……的打击；……受到的重创

`blow` 本义是身体上的一击，引申为任何使某事物受损、倒退的冲击性事件。固定搭配 `a blow to/for sth.`，介词用 `to` 表示打击对象是受损方；`deal/strike a blow to`（给……以打击）是动词扩展形式。`a blow to one's reputation/confidence/economy` 是高频组合。

**核心意象：** 不是慢慢磨损，是一拳打实的那种损伤。

1. The sudden resignation of three outside directors in a single week dealt a severe blow to investor confidence, sending the company's share price down nearly fifteen percent.
2. Leaving before the scandal surfaced shielded her from the blow to her reputation that felled most of her former colleagues on the board.
3. Note the direction of damage: *a blow to the economy* means the economy suffers; compare *a boost to the economy*, same structure, opposite effect — the preposition `to` stays the same regardless of direction.
