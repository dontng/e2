# Day 140 · 2026-09-24

## 原句（Input）

> A survey by Barna research group found the top reason given by Americans for the fake news phenomenon is “reader error,” more so than made-up stories or factual mistakes in reporting.

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>found ... is：调查发现的是一整个判断</big></big></strong></summary>

> A survey by Barna research group **found** the top reason given by Americans for the fake news phenomenon **is “reader error”** ...

Keep both actions, then expose the boundary:

> A survey **found that** the top reason **is “reader error.”**
>
> A survey **found [the top reason given by Americans for the fake news phenomenon is “reader error”].**

The survey found a **claim** about the reason. <code>is</code> belongs inside what the survey found; it does not give the survey a second action. 你已经写出“调研……发现……是读者错误”，可以从这里继续；不要在 <code>found</code> 后立即把 <code>the top reason</code> 译成“一个大的原因”，而让中间两层关系失去位置。

<code>top reason</code> means the leading reason among answers, not a physically big one. The statement reports how Americans explain the phenomenon; it does not prove that reader error objectively causes fake news. 先对：调查发现〔受访者的首要归因是读者错误〕。再准：<code>top</code> 是“首要”，归因主体是受访者。
</details>

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>两次 by、一次 for：把同一 reason 接完整</big></big></strong></summary>

Keep the inner claim in view while restoring one part at a time:

> **A survey by Barna research group** found the top reason is “reader error.”
>
> A survey by Barna research group found the top **reason given by Americans** is “reader error.”
>
> A survey by Barna research group found the top reason given by Americans **for the fake news phenomenon** is “reader error.”

The first <code>by</code> stays with <code>survey</code>: who conducted it? The second stays with <code>reason given</code>: who supplied that reason? <code>for the fake news phenomenon</code> stays with <code>reason</code>: what does the reason explain? 你写“一份来自 Barna 的调研组织”和“对由美国人对……”，就是这几块没有接稳。先把英语中的邻接关系恢复，再写中文，不必给两个 <code>by</code> 配同一个中文词。

> A survey **by Barna** found a reason **given by Americans** **for fake news**.

This shorter version preserves all three attachments. The <code>reason</code> was given by Americans **for** the phenomenon; the <code>survey</code> was conducted by Barna. “Americans gave the reason” does not mean “Americans conducted the survey.” 这里用英语说清“谁做调查、谁给解释、解释什么”，无需列出从句名称。

<details>
<summary><strong>小词复盘：<code>reason for X</code> 的 X 怎样变化</strong></summary>

> People offered a reason **for the delay**.
>
> People offered a reason **for the change in policy**.
>
> People offered a reason **for the fake news phenomenon**.

X changes; the question stays “What needs explaining?” <code>for</code> here connects a reason to the thing explained; it does not describe a purpose (“为了”). 再看新句：<code>The board gave a reason for the closure.</code> 谁给理由？哪件事等待解释？答出后回原句，把 <code>given by Americans</code> 和 <code>for ...</code> 同时接在 <code>reason</code> 上。
</details>
</details>

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>more so than：到底什么比什么更常被归因</big></big></strong></summary>

> ... the top reason ... is **“reader error,” more so than made-up stories or factual mistakes in reporting.**

Try saying the comparison in fuller English:

> Americans gave **reader error** as the leading explanation for fake news, **more often than they gave made-up stories or factual mistakes in reporting** as the explanation.

This makes the implied comparison visible. <code>so</code> points back to the preceding judgment; it is not a new thing called “更多如此”。你已经读出“读者错误”与句尾两项，却在“更多如此，多于……”处丢掉同一把比较尺子。核对：三项都在“被用来解释假新闻”的位置；句子没有给出具体百分比。

The two alternatives are not interchangeable. <code>made-up stories</code> can be invented from the start; <code>factual mistakes in reporting</code> are errors in reported facts. 如果把两项写成“粉饰的事件”和“意外的错误”，即使保住了 <code>more ... than</code>，比较的内容仍会错。

<details>
<summary><strong>词的变化：<code>make up → made-up</code>；<code>fact → factual</code></strong></summary>

> She **made up** an excuse for missing the meeting.
>
> It was a **made-up** excuse; no such meeting had been scheduled.
>
> The report contained **made-up stories** alongside real interviews.

The action <code>make up</code> can mean inventing a story or excuse; <code>made-up</code> describes the product of that invention. 中文落点是“编造的”，不是“粉饰的”。<code>makeup</code> can mean cosmetics, but its spelling and use differ from <code>made-up stories</code>. 你的“粉饰”可能借用了化妆的熟义，也可能只是就地猜词；不能据此判定真实检索路径。观察完整搭配 <code>made-up + story/excuse</code>，比硬造一个从化妆走到编造的词源故事可靠。

> The reporter made a **factual** mistake: the meeting was on Tuesday, not Thursday.
>
> The reporter made an **accidental** mistake: she hit the wrong key while typing.
>
> The article's **factual mistakes in reporting** were corrected later.

<code>factual</code> tracks <code>fact</code>: facts such as dates, numbers and names; <code>accidental</code> describes whether an error was unintended. A factual mistake may also be accidental, but the original commits only to its subject, not its cause. <code>reporting</code> here is the work of reporting news. 本句先要把“编造”与“报道中事实弄错”放回并列两项，不能从 <code>factual</code> 推出“意外”。

复盘时先遮住中文，在新句 <code>The witness gave a made-up account, while the newspaper corrected a factual mistake in its report</code> 中辨认：哪一项是编出来的，哪一项是事实出错？再回原句检查比较方向。
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>省略可展开：<code>more so than</code> 在新场景里怎样接回前面</strong></summary>

> The committee blamed **poor planning**, more so than **bad weather**.
>
> The committee blamed poor planning **more than it blamed bad weather**.

The second version opens up what the first leaves understood. In the original, return to the judgment about the **top reason given by Americans**; then read <code>made-up stories or factual mistakes</code> as the two alternatives. 不必逐字译出 <code>so</code>，但必须让“更倾向归因于谁”在中文里可见。
</details>

## 把比较完整交成中文

先对：调查发现的是受访者的首要归因；“读者错误”与另外两种归因放在同一比较中。

> 巴纳研究集团开展的一项调查发现，美国人解释假新闻现象时，最常给出的原因是“读者错误”，而不是编造的新闻故事或新闻报道中的事实性错误。

再准：<code>top</code> 指首要，<code>more so than</code> 指相对更常被归因，不能让调查替受访者证明客观成因。若中文希望保留“更倾向”的语气：

> 巴纳研究集团的一项调查发现，对于假新闻现象，美国人更倾向于归咎于“读者错误”，而非编造的新闻故事或报道中的事实性错误。
