# Day 141 · 2026-09-25

## 原句（Input）

> It is against that background that the information commissioner, Elizabeth Denham, has issued her damning verdict against the Royal Free hospital trust under the NHS, which handed over to DeepMind the records of 1.6 million patients in 2015 on the basis of a vague agreement which took far too little account of the patients’ rights and their expectations of privacy.

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>先让主句落地：谁对谁作出了什么判断</big></big></strong></summary>

> **It is against that background that** the information commissioner, Elizabeth Denham, **has issued** her damning verdict **against** the Royal Free hospital trust under the NHS, **which** handed over ... **which** took ...

Temporarily leave aside the opening emphasis and the two later <code>which</code> passages. Continue until you have a complete action:

> **The information commissioner has issued her verdict against the hospital trust.**

Who acted? The commissioner. What did she issue? A verdict. Who was it against? The hospital trust. 你首译有“ED”“提出”“皇家自由医院”，这些位置并非全丢；但“信息承接者／糟糕的策略／信条”让三个承重词共同改变了事件。它们要在这里修正，不能等到文章末尾再列词表。

Now restore the opening without changing that action:

> Denham issued a verdict against the trust **against that background**.
>
> **It is against that background that** Denham issued a verdict against the trust.

The first <code>that</code> points to a background already mentioned; the second brings us back to the action being highlighted. 句首并不是“反对背景的信息承接者”。读到第二个 <code>that</code>，继续等 <code>the information commissioner ... has issued</code>；认得它是强调句可以节省说明，但无需先判定强调了哪种语法成分才敢读主句。

<details>
<summary><strong>承重词：<code>commissioner</code>、<code>verdict</code>、<code>trust</code> 如何回到事件</strong></summary>

> The **commissioner** issued a **verdict** against the **trust**.
>
> The **commissioner** issued a **plan** for the **trust**.

A <code>commissioner</code> is an official assigned public duties; here the title is <code>information commissioner</code>，可暂记“信息专员”。<code>verdict</code> is a judgment or assessment: in court it may be a legal verdict; here a <code>damning verdict</code> is a strongly unfavorable assessment. <code>issue</code> can accompany both <code>a plan</code> and <code>a verdict</code>; the object tells you what was issued. 你写“策略”会把“评价机构”误变为“提出方案”。若 <code>commissioner</code> 暂不会，可先占“〔某官员〕”，仍保住她是判断的发出者。

<code>trust</code> deserves a bridge from the familiar use:

> Patients **trust** their doctor. — They believe the doctor will act responsibly.
>
> They placed the funds **in trust**. — Someone was entrusted to hold or manage them.
>
> A hospital **trust** manages hospitals and can make decisions about records.

The first is a person's belief; the second moves toward **entrusting responsibility**; the third names an organization charged with responsibilities. The bridge helps you remember why English can use the same word; it does not claim an NHS hospital trust is a private property trust. In the original, <code>the ... hospital trust under the NHS</code> names an institution, and the next <code>which handed over ... the records</code> makes it an actor. 中文可记为“皇家自由医院管理机构”，不是“信条”。<code>Royal Free</code> 是专名，保留原名。

首译“信条”证明机构义没到位，尚不能断定是熟义抢答、形近误认，还是没见过英国机构名称。复盘不靠重复背译名：先遮住解释，读 <code>The trust approved a new policy for the hospital</code>。这个 <code>trust</code> 能批准政策吗？若能，当前是人对人的信任，还是能办事的机构？再回原句用 <code>handed over</code> 验证。

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>装回力度、身份和两处 against</big></big></strong></summary>

Keep the same judgment and return one layer at a time:

> The information commissioner, **Elizabeth Denham**, has issued her verdict against the hospital trust.
>
> The information commissioner, Elizabeth Denham, has issued her **damning verdict against the Royal Free hospital trust under the NHS**.
>
> **It is against that background that** the information commissioner, Elizabeth Denham, has issued her damning verdict against the Royal Free hospital trust under the NHS.

Elizabeth Denham identifies the commissioner; <code>damning</code> describes how severe the criticism is, not how poor the verdict's quality is. <code>under the NHS</code> places the hospital trust in the NHS system. 你首译的“NHS 之下”抓到层级，但要让层级挂到机构，不是挂到抽象“信条”。

The two <code>against</code>s do different work because they attach to different things:

> **Against that background**, the verdict makes sense.
>
> The commissioner issued a verdict **against the trust**.

The first places an event in its background; the second identifies the target of an unfavorable verdict. 把两处都译成“反对”，就得不到这两条英文关系。

<details>
<summary><strong>小词复盘：先看 <code>against</code> 贴着谁</strong></summary>

> Against that background, **the decision** was understandable.
>
> The decision **against the company** was severe.

The first phrase frames the whole decision in context; the second connects an adverse decision to its target. 新句 <code>Against that background, a ruling against the company surprised few people</code> 同时出现两次；先说各自贴谁，再译“在那样的背景下／对公司不利的裁定”。本句两处也照此核对。
</details>
</details>

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>第一个 which：机构移交了资料，DeepMind 是接收者</big></big></strong></summary>

> ... the Royal Free hospital trust under the NHS, **which handed over to DeepMind the records of 1.6 million patients in 2015** ...

The first <code>which</code> picks up the trust, an institution capable of handing over records. Preserve the original order and wait for the thing handed over:

> The trust handed over **to DeepMind** ... （交给谁已有答案；交出了什么还没有。）
>
> The trust handed over **to DeepMind the records of 1.6 million patients**.
>
> The trust handed over **the records of 1.6 million patients to DeepMind**.

The last sentence checks the same roles in a more familiar order. Your <code>hand over</code> and “160 万……的数据” show parts of this event survived, but “hand over 掉深度思考” loses the receiver and leaves the records outside the action. <code>DeepMind</code> is a name: keep it in English if uncertain. <code>in 2015</code> dates the handover.

<details>
<summary><strong>词形：<code>patients</code> / <code>parents</code>；数字和 records 怎样落位</strong></summary>

> The hospital held records for 1.6 million **patients**.
>
> The school contacted 1.6 million **parents**.

<code>patients</code> are people receiving medical care（患者）；<code>parents</code> are mothers and fathers（父母）。你写“父母”，能确定对象被读错；是否看错具体字母，还须下次并排核实。<code>records of patients</code> in this hospital setting are patient records（患者资料／病历），而不是“成绩记录”。<code>1.6 million</code> = 160 万，数的是患者。新句 <code>The clinic transferred the records of 60,000 patients to a new database</code>：转移的是人、资料，还是数据库？先认清字母，再把角色接回动词。
</details>
</details>

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>依据什么移交：协议属于这一次 handover</big></big></strong></summary>

> The trust handed over the records to DeepMind **in 2015 on the basis of a vague agreement**.

The trust handed over the records; <code>in 2015</code> locates the action in time; <code>on the basis of a vague agreement</code> states the basis on which it happened. 你首译读到了“2015”“模糊协议基础”，却还需将它们接在同一次移交上，别把“协议”接成资料本身。

> The committee made a decision **on the basis of a report**.
>
> The trust handed over the records **on the basis of an agreement**.

The phrase names what an action rests on; <code>vague</code> makes the agreement insufficiently clear. The word alone does not establish that it was illegal. 第二个 <code>which</code> 才说明协议具体忽略了什么。

<details>
<summary><strong>短复盘：<code>on the basis of</code> 和动作一起取出</strong></summary>

读 <code>They shared the data on the basis of an agreement</code>，先找到 shared，再问依据是什么；回原句换成 <code>handed over</code>，答案仍是 agreement。不要把 <code>on the basis of</code> 单独背成脱离动作的一串中文。
</details>
</details>

<details open>
<summary><strong><big><big>第二个 which：协议忽略了患者的两项关切</big></big></strong></summary>

> ... on the basis of **a vague agreement which took far too little account of the patients’ rights and their expectations of privacy**.

The second <code>which</code> follows <code>agreement</code>; now the agreement is what took too little account of something. Keep the same frame and add the objects one at a time:

> The agreement took account of **the patients’ rights**.
>
> The agreement took account of **the patients’ rights and their expectations of privacy**.
>
> The agreement took **far too little** account of the patients’ rights and their expectations of privacy.

The last change reverses the judgment: the agreement gave these concerns much less attention than it should have. <code>their</code> still refers to the patients. 你首译止于“模糊协议”，两项权益和程度都没有交出；即使一时不认识 <code>take account of</code>，也可先保住“协议如何对待〔患者权利 + 隐私期待〕”的框架，之后把核心动作补准。

<details>
<summary><strong>词组怎样变：<code>account</code> 在这句话里不等于账户</strong></summary>

> The plan **took account of** patients’ rights.
>
> The plan **took too little account of** patients’ rights.
>
> The plan **took far too little account of** patients’ rights.

With <code>take account of X</code>, X is **taken into consideration**（纳入考虑）；<code>too little</code> makes the consideration inadequate, and <code>far</code> strengthens that inadequacy. A bank <code>account</code> is another familiar use of the word, but <code>take ... account of X</code> is a recognizable phrase about considering X; forcing the banking sense into it gives no coherent action. <code>on account of X</code> can mean “因为 X”; the original begins with <code>took</code> and describes insufficient consideration.

复盘线索：看到 <code>took ... account of X</code>，先找 X 和程度。新句 <code>The policy took too little account of local families’ needs</code>：顾及了谁，程度如何？回本句核对两项并列：患者权利，以及他们对隐私的期待。不可加强为“完全没有保障”。
</details>
</details>

## 把三层事件交成中文

先对：专员评断机构；机构移交资料；协议未充分顾及两项关切。

> 正是在这样的背景下，信息专员伊丽莎白·德纳姆对 NHS 体系下的皇家自由医院管理机构作出了严厉评断。该机构于 2015 年依据一份含糊的协议，将 160 万名患者的资料移交给 DeepMind；该协议远未充分顾及患者的权利和他们对隐私保护的期待。

再准：句首的背景来自前文，不另编内容；<code>damning</code> 是评断的严厉程度，<code>far too little</code> 是顾及不足的严重程度；两个 <code>which</code> 分别接机构与协议。更自然的中文可以连成：

> 正是在这样的背景下，信息专员伊丽莎白·德纳姆严厉批评了 NHS 体系下的皇家自由医院管理机构：该机构曾于 2015 年依据一份含糊不清的协议，将 160 万名患者的病历资料交给 DeepMind，而协议远未充分顾及患者的权利及其对隐私保护的期待。
