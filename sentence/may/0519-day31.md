> But brain researchers have discovered that when we consciously develop new habits, we create parallel paths, and even entirely new brain cells, that can jump our trains of thought onto new, innovative tracks.

     jump onto = 跃上，tracks = 轨道（目的地），innovative 修饰的是这条轨道。

## Vocab

### consciously *(adv.)*

> With awareness — actions done on purpose after careful consideration.

When you do something consciously, you do it on purpose, after thinking seriously about it. A kindergarten teacher might consciously choose a soothing book to calm his class down at story time. If you do something instinctively or automatically, you're not consciously choosing to do it — it's just happening. On the other hand, if you consciously invite only friends who get along with each other to your party, you've done it in a thoughtful, intentional way. The adverb consciously comes from conscious, "aware and awake," from the Latin *conscius*, "knowing or aware," which has a Greek root, *syneidos*, "to know."

---

### innovative *(adj.)*

> Something innovative is new and original. If you love to experiment and find new ways to do things, you are an innovative person.

Innovative, like *nova*, *novel*, and *novice*, comes from the Latin *novus*, which means "new." Something innovative renews or alters the way something has been done. You can use innovative to describe the thing or the person that made it. If your English teacher objects to your experimental writing style, tell him, "I'm an innovative writer making innovative sentences!" Antonyms are *unoriginal* and *hackneyed*.

---

### track *(n.)*

> a line or route along which something travels or moves

If you document your study time and record all your exam scores, then you track your progress in school. In this case the verb track shows that you're following the path of something. The noun track can refer to a path of a more literal kind.

---

## Phrases

### innovative tracks

`innovative` *(adj.)* + `tracks` *(n.pl.)* — 创新性的轨道

整句用了一个隐喻：思维像火车（*trains of thought*），新习惯让它跳上全新的轨道（*jump onto new, innovative tracks*）。这里的 tracks 不是"追踪"，而是火车轨道，指思维运行的方向和路径。*innovative* 修饰的是这条轨道本身——是新的、从未走过的方向。

## 问答收录

### Q1：参考译文里的"会"从哪来？`that` 为什么指的是 brain cells？

**关于"会"**

原文 `we create` 是一般现在时，表达的是"当条件成立时，自然发生的结果"。中文里"会"用来表达这种"条件→必然结果"的关系很自然——"当我们培养新习惯时，**会**形成通路"。去掉"会"也通，但少了那种"这是自然会发生的事"的语气。"会"在这里不是将来时，是表示倾向和必然性。

**关于 `that` 指谁**

`that` 指的是 **parallel paths 和 brain cells 两者**，不只是 brain cells。依据有两个：

第一，`and even` 把两个名词并列成一组：`parallel paths`, and even `entirely new brain cells`——`and even` 在强调递进，不是引出另一件事，所以这两个名词是一个整体，`that` 接的是整组。

第二，逻辑上两者都能"跳轨"——通路和脑细胞都是大脑里发生的改变，都能重新引导思维走向。所以译成"**它们**能将……"用复数，指两者。

---

### Q2：为什么不能写"研究者们有发现到"？

用"有+动词"来对应 `have done`，思路本身没错，但这是**台湾中文/南方方言**的用法，不是普通话书面语的标准。台湾中文里很常见（我有去、你有没有吃），但大陆书面普通话里这个结构不成立。

英语的 `have done` 在中文里没有固定对应，要看强调的是什么：

| 强调点 | 中文处理 | 例子 |
|--------|----------|---------|
| 动作完成了 | 了 | 发现了 |
| 已经完成 | 已经 | 已经发现 |
| 有过这个经历 | 过 | 发现过 |
| 在从句里引出后文 | 通常不加标记，直接"发现" | 研究者发现，当…… |

这句话的重点在后面的 that 从句，所以直接说"发现"就够了。

另外，"发现到"也不对——"发现"本身已包含"察觉到结果"的意思，不需要再加"到"。对比：看到、听到、想到（这些动词本身没有"达到结果"的含义，加"到"才完整）。所以"有发现到"同时有两处问题。
